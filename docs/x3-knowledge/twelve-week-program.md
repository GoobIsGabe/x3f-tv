## 2. CONFIRMED — PROGRAM IDENTITY

`CONFIRMED` The X3 12-Week Program is the **default, included-with-purchase** program that every new X3 owner starts on. It is distinct from the X3 Hypertrophy Program (designed by Matt Wenning, higher volume, supersets, ~1 hour sessions) and from the optional Westside Barbell X3 Training Academy membership. *(A3, A13)*

`CONFIRMED` Stated time commitment: **10–20 minutes per day**. *(A3)*

`CONFIRMED` Structure: a **push/pull split**, **one set per exercise taken to complete fatigue**, with video instruction for every movement. *(A3)*

`CONFIRMED` Stated purpose, in Jaquish Biomedical's own framing, is efficiency — getting strong and lean in the least time — as opposed to the Hypertrophy Program's focus on maximum size. *(A13)*

## 3. CONFIRMED — THE TWO-PHASE STRUCTURE

`CONFIRMED` The program splits into exactly **two phases**: **Weeks 1–4** and **Weeks 5–12**. There is no third phase. *(A3, B1, C4)*

### Phase 1 — Weeks 1–4

`CONFIRMED` **4 training days, 3 rest days per week.** *(A3, B1)*

| Day 1 | Day 2 | Day 3 | Day 4 | Day 5 | Day 6 | Day 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Push** | **Pull** | Rest | **Push** | **Pull** | Rest | Rest |

`CONFIRMED` This layout is printed identically on the official preview page (A3) and the affiliate mirror (B1).

`CONFIRMED` Stated purpose of Phase 1: let the body acclimate to a training method that requires taking muscles to complete fatigue — something the materials describe as effectively unattainable with free weights. *(B1)*

`CONFIRMED` Net effect: **each movement is trained 2× per week** during Phase 1.

### Phase 2 — Weeks 5–12

`CONFIRMED` **6 training days, 1 rest day per week.** *(A3, B1)*

| Day 1 | Day 2 | Day 3 | Day 4 | Day 5 | Day 6 | Day 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Push** | **Pull** | **Push** | **Pull** | **Push** | **Pull** | Rest |

`CONFIRMED` Net effect: **each movement is trained 3× per week**. *(A7 — "muscles are targeted three times per week")*

`CONFIRMED` The recovery rationale for the 6-day split, in X3's own words: alternating push and pull without overlap gives each muscle group **48 hours or longer** before it is trained again, against a claimed ideal recovery window of **36 hours** (protein synthesis said to peak at 24h and return to baseline at 36h). *(A7)*

### What actually changes between Phase 1 and Phase 2

`CONFIRMED` **1. Frequency.** 4 sessions/week → 6 sessions/week. Rest days go 3 → 1. This is the headline change. *(A3, B1)*

`CONFIRMED` **2. Movement variations become available at Week 5.** From Week 5 the trainee may:
- **Replace Front Squat with Split Squat** (unilateral — one leg at a time, for deeper fatigue per limb), and
- **Add Pec Crossover** to Push Day.

Both are described as optional progressions ("you can consider…", "may begin incorporating…") rather than hard swaps. *(B1, C4)*

`CONFIRMED` **3. Technique emphasis, Weeks 5–8.** The focus shifts to mastering three named principles: **Constant Tension**, **Diminishing Range**, and **Complete Fatigue**. *(A3, B1)*

`CONFIRMED` **4. Advanced technique unlock, after Week 8 (i.e. Weeks 9–12).** Dr. Jaquish introduces **breath control** and **stabilization firing** — engaging supporting musculature for strength, balance and efficiency. *(B1)*

> This gives a useful three-beat mental model for Phase 2 even though the program formally has two phases:
> Weeks 5–8 = frequency + the three principles. Weeks 9–12 = breath control + stabilization firing.

## 4. CONFIRMED — MOVEMENT ORDER (read this section carefully)

### Push Day — order

`CONFIRMED` **1. Chest Press → 2. Tricep Press → 3. Overhead Press → 4. Front Squat**

Corroboration: this exact sequence appears in **five** independent places — the official Workout Card PDF's reading order (A1), the affiliate program preview which prints it as an ordered list (B1), a full 12-week independent user log (C1), Garage Gym Reviews (C2), and Joe Cannon's review (C3). No public source gives a different Push Day order.

### Pull Day — order

`CONFIRMED` **1. Deadlift → 2. Bent Row → 3. Bicep Curl → 4. Calf Raise**

Corroboration: B1 (printed as an ordered list), C1, C2, C3 — four independent sources in exact agreement.

> ### ⚠️ TRAP #1 — the Workout Card's grid is NOT the order
> The official Workout Card (A1) lays each day out as a **2×2 photo grid**, not a numbered list. On the Pull Day card the grid positions are:
> `top-left Deadlift | top-right Bicep Curl` / `bottom-left Bent Row | bottom-right Calf Raise`.
> Read left-to-right that yields Deadlift → Bicep Curl → Bent Row → Calf Raise, which **contradicts** every text source. The card is a *which-movement-goes-on-which-day* reference, not a sequence. **Use the four-source text order above.** (On the Push Day card the grid happens to agree with the text order, which is probably why the discrepancy has gone unnoticed.)

> ### ⚠️ TRAP #2 — "largest muscle group first" does NOT describe X3's Push Day
> This heuristic is commonly assumed and it is **wrong for X3 Push Day**. The Front Squat — by far the largest-muscle movement of the day — is prescribed **last**, after chest, triceps and shoulders.
> - **Pull Day** *does* happen to run roughly largest→smallest: Deadlift → Bent Row → Bicep Curl → Calf Raise.
> - **Push Day** does not: Chest Press → Tricep Press → Overhead Press → Front Squat.
>
> Do not let an app "correct" the Push Day order to put squats first. That is not the program.

### The stated rationale for Push Day ordering

`LIKELY` X3's published rationale is **multi-joint before single-joint**, not largest-muscle-first: the Chest Press leads because it is a compound movement, and you do not want to pre-exhaust the triceps (a smaller muscle that assists the press) before the compound movement that depends on them.

*Confidence note:* this rationale was recovered from a search-engine snippet of the official X3 Chest Press form page (formerly `https://www.jaquishbiomedical.com/x3-program/exercise-form/x3-chest-press/`, now 301'd behind the login wall at `https://programs.jaquishbiomedical.com/x3-exercises/chest-press`). The page itself could not be loaded to verify. Treat as X3's stated reasoning, not as verified quotation.

`UNCERTAIN` Why **Tricep Press precedes Overhead Press** is not explained anywhere public. Under a pure multi-joint-first rule the Overhead Press (compound) would come before the Tricep Press (isolation). The published order puts the isolation movement second. No public source resolves this. **Follow the order as published; do not rationalize it in-app.**

## 5. CONFIRMED — THE EIGHT STANDARD MOVEMENTS

`CONFIRMED` X3 names eight standard exercises with these primary muscle groups *(A8)*:

| Exercise | Primary muscles | Day (per A1) |
|---|---|---|
| Overhead Press *(alt: Upright Row)* | Deltoids | Push |
| Chest Press *(and Pec Crossover)* | Pectorals | Push |
| Tricep Press | Triceps | Push |
| Front Squat *(and X3 Split Squat)* | Abdominals, glutes, quadriceps | Push |
| Deadlift | Trapezius, hamstrings | Pull |
| Bent Row | Latissimus dorsi | Pull |
| Bicep Curl | Biceps | Pull |
| Calf Raise | Calves, hamstrings | Pull |

`CONFIRMED` Push Day trains primarily **chest, triceps, quads, deltoids**. Pull Day trains primarily **traps, lats, glutes, hamstrings, biceps, calves**. *(B2)*

`CONFIRMED` Setup notes from the Workout Card (A1), useful for in-app cueing:
- **Chest Press** — band doubled, wrapped around shoulders below the deltoids; press forward and slightly downward, lats engaged, elbows flared.
- **Tricep Press** — band doubled, wrapped around shoulders slightly *below* the chest-press position; hinge at the elbow only, elbows tucked.
- **Overhead Press** — band under the ground plate; press to just short of full extension; lower to chin level.
- **Front Squat** — band under the ground plate; bar rests on top of the shoulders; band travels a straight vertical path.
- **Deadlift** — band doubled, under the ground plate; shoulders pulled back; straight vertical band path.
- **Bent Row** — band doubled, under the ground plate; bar pulled up and into the lower abdomen.
- **Bicep Curl** — band under the ground plate; bar lifted straight up along the body.
- **Calf Raise** — band doubled, under the ground plate; **ball of the foot** over the band channel (all other movements use midfoot).

`CONFIRMED` **Upright Row is a substitution, not a program movement.** X3 states the upright row is inferior to the overhead press (less deltoid shortening) and is intended only for people who cannot press overhead pain-free due to shoulder damage. *(A11)*

`CONFIRMED` **No direct ab work exists in the 12-week program.** X3's position is that the deadlift and front squat activate the abdominals more effectively than isolation ab exercises, because the abs function as stabilizers and the resistance rises into the stronger ranges. *(A9)*

`CONFIRMED` **Split Squat is the modern default when using the X3 Force bar.** The X3 Force app defaults to Split Squats rather than Front Squats, because in a front squat the shoulders press against the bar hooks and corrupt the force readings. Front Squat remains selectable for users with limitations that rule out split squats. *(A14)*

## 6. CONFIRMED — SET, REP AND TEMPO PROTOCOL

Straight from the official Quick Start Guide (A2), corroborated by A5/A6/B2:

`CONFIRMED` **One set per exercise.** Not 3×10. One set. *(A2 rule 2, A6)*

`CONFIRMED` **15–40 complete repetitions**, then **as many partial reps as possible**, continuing "until you can no longer move the bar even an inch." Low-rep free-weight tactics are explicitly rejected. *(A2 rule 2)*

`CONFIRMED` **Tempo: 2–3 seconds up, 2–3 seconds down.** *(A2 rule 4, A5)*

`CONFIRMED` **Constant tension.** Never let the band go slack at the bottom; never lock out joints at the top. *(A2 rule 3)*

`CONFIRMED` **Rest between exercises: "only long enough to catch your breath."** No numeric duration is published for the 12-Week Program. *(A6)*
> Contrast: the *Hypertrophy* Program does specify numbers — 20 seconds between exercises within a superset, 2 minutes between superset pairs. Do not apply those to the 12-Week Program. *(A13)*

`CONFIRMED` **No warm-up is prescribed.** X3's stated position is that the first few reps serve as the warm-up. Post-workout stretching is recommended. *(A10)*

`CONFIRMED` **Rationale for one set:** variable resistance is claimed to fatigue the muscle across all ranges in a single set. Multiple sets are said to give the muscle a chance to rest, blunting the growth signal. The single set is described as producing myofibrillar fatigue in the strong range and sarcoplasmic fatigue (ATP/glycogen depletion) in the diminished range. *(A6)*

`CONFIRMED` **Do not add exercises.** X3 states that if the movements are performed correctly you will be too fatigued to add more, and advises completing the full 12 weeks before layering on any other program. *(A12)*

## 7. CONFIRMED — BAND PROGRESSION RULES

These are the load-management rules, and they are unusually crisp. From the Quick Start Guide (A2), rules 5 and 6:

`CONFIRMED` **Starting load:** start with the **lightest band for all exercises**. *(A2 rule 5)*

`CONFIRMED` **Move UP a band when you can complete 40 slow, controlled repetitions with good form.** Partial reps do not count toward the 40. *(A2 rule 5; A5; B2)*

`CONFIRMED` **Move DOWN a band if you cannot complete 15 slow, controlled repetitions.** *(A2 rule 6)*

`CONFIRMED` Corollary stated by X3: it is **counterproductive** to pick a heavier band and fail at fewer reps. The protocol deliberately favours a lighter band with more reps (up to 40) to fatigue the maximum number of fibres. *(A5)*

`CONFIRMED` Band selection is **per-exercise, not per-athlete.** A trainee will be on different bands for different movements simultaneously — this is normal and expected. The independent 12-week log (C1) records the chest press reaching the dark grey band by week 7 while the deadlift did not reach light grey until week 9, i.e. wildly different progression rates on the same body.

`UNCERTAIN` **Band colour names and their resistance ranges in pounds.** Not recovered from any public source in this pass. One official article mentions "540 pounds at peak" for the elite band on a chest press (A5), but no ordered colour→poundage table was found. The community-referenced ordering (ultra-light / white / light grey / dark grey / black / elite) appears in third-party writing but was **not** verified against an official page here. Do not hard-code a band ladder from this document.

> ### ⚠️ TRAP #3 — the 40-rep gate is a *form* gate, not a rep target
> "40 slow and controlled reps with good form" is the trigger to increase resistance. It is not a workout goal to chase. The workout goal is complete fatigue in one set. An app that shows "40 reps" as a target will push users toward speed and sloppy range.

## 8. DELOAD — WHAT THE EVIDENCE ACTUALLY SAYS

`CONFIRMED` **No deload week appears anywhere in the public description of the X3 12-Week Program.** The only scheduled recovery is the weekly rest day pattern: 3 rest days/week in Weeks 1–4, 1 rest day/week in Weeks 5–12. No source describes a lighter week, a reduced-volume week, or a planned break at any point in the 12 weeks. *(A3, A7, B1, C1)*

`CONFIRMED` The 12-week program's recovery model is **intra-week, not inter-week**: the push/pull alternation is itself the recovery mechanism, giving each muscle group ≥48 hours. *(A7)*

`CONFIRMED` X3's stated position leans against needing scheduled downtime — it claims users "won't need to keep taking time off to heal sore joints and damaged connective tissue" because of reduced joint loading. *(A7)*

`UNCERTAIN` Whether the gated program content contains any deload, back-off week, or "reset" instruction not surfaced in marketing. **Cannot be ruled out without logging in.** An app should say "no deload is publicly documented" rather than "there is no deload."

`CONFIRMED` (context, not prescription) The independent 12-week log (C1) reports that the 6×/week Phase 2 frequency produced overtraining symptoms after ~3 weeks and the user unilaterally dropped to 2×/week. This is one person's experience and is **not** part of the program — but it is a known real-world failure mode of the Phase 1→2 jump and worth designing around (e.g. an app might surface a "how are you recovering?" check-in at Week 7–8).
