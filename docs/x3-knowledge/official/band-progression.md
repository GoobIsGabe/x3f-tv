# Band progression — when and how to graduate to the next X3 band

**Researched:** 2026-09-08. **Question:** the app is about to tell a user to change how they
train. What does Jaquish Biomedical *actually say*, and what is it silent on?

This file supplements `12-week-program.md` (which holds the member-site "Core X3 Training
Principles") and `exercises.md`. Where the member-site principle text and this file appear to
differ, they do not — this file records **additional** official sources that the member site
does not contain.

## 2. The rule, restated from three independent official statements

**SOURCED — Quick Start Guide, printed insert, items 5 and 6:**

> "Only move to the next heavier band when you can complete 40 slow and controlled repetitions"

> "Move down in resistance to a lighter band if you cannot perform 15 slow and controlled
> repetitions."

Item 5 opens with **"Start with the lightest band for all exercises."**

**SOURCED — support article "How do I use the X3 Bar Elite?"** reproduces the twelve Quick Start
Guide items word for word. The instruction is therefore stated identically in print and online.

**SOURCED — Dr Jaquish, Radio Athens interview:**

> "We recommend 15 repetitions, and then if you can go to 40 repetitions then you just move to
> the next heavier band."

The word **"just"** is the tell: in his own framing this is immediate, not conditional on a
streak.

**DERIVED — the ceiling matters.** The support article "What bands should I use?" says the
program uses "a minimum of 15 repetitions and a maximum of 40", and the pick-a-band article says
"On the maximum side, we go up to 40 repetitions." So 40 is a **cap**, not an open-ended count.
In practice "40 or more full reps" and "reached 40 full reps" are the same event, and the app
should treat 40 as the point where full reps stop and partials begin — not as a number to exceed.

## 3. Q1 — Consistency: once, or repeatedly?

**Both answers exist in official copy, from different parts of the company.**

### 3a. The instructional sources say ONCE (no streak requirement)

**SOURCED — Quick Start Guide item 5:** "when you can complete 40 slow and controlled
repetitions with good form". No frequency qualifier.

**SOURCED — support, "What bands should I use? How many reps should I do?":**

> "You are ready to move up a band when you can do 40 slow and controlled reps"

**SOURCED — Dr Jaquish, Radio Athens:** "if you can go to 40 repetitions then you just move to
the next heavier band."

**SOURCED — the member-site principle already in `12-week-program.md`:** "Don't move up until you
can perform 40 slow, controlled full range reps with good form."

Four official statements, none of which require more than one qualifying performance.

### 3b. The commerce sources say CONSISTENTLY

**SOURCED — X3 Light Grey Band product page:**

> "When you can consistently reach 40 full reps with the X3 White Band"

and the same page's summary bullet:

> "move up when you can perform 40 full reps on any exercise"

**SOURCED — X3 Dark Grey Band product page:**

> "When you can consistently reach 40 full reps with the X3 Light Grey Band"

**SOURCED — X3 Black Band product page:**

> "Move up to it when you can consistently reach 40 full reps with the X3 Dark Grey Band."

**SOURCED — X3 Elite Band product page:**

> "when you can complete the full 15–40 rep protocol with the X3 Black Band across your
> exercises"

### 3c. What is and is not established

- **SOURCED:** the word "consistently" is official Jaquish Biomedical copy, on three band pages.
- **UNCONFIRMED:** *how many* sessions "consistently" means. **No official source anywhere
  quantifies it.** Nothing says "two workouts in a row", "two sessions", "every time", or
  "each session". Searching for those phrasings across the support centre, the PDFs, the product
  pages and the podcast transcripts returns nothing.
- **DERIVED:** the two families of wording are not contradictory so much as differently
  motivated. The instructional copy is telling a beginner how to train; the product copy is
  telling a shopper when to spend $25. The commerce copy is the *more conservative* of the two
  and is the one that protects the user from a bad day producing a wrong recommendation.
- **DERIVED:** the safest reading that violates neither is **"the first 40-rep session is the
  official trigger; a second one is the confirmation."** See §11.

> **The app must never say "the program says do it twice."** No source says that.

## 4. Q2 — Per movement or global?

**Answer: per movement, and this is now well sourced from several independent directions.**

**SOURCED — Quick Start Guide item 5:** "Start with the lightest band **for all exercises**."
Per-exercise band assignment is the starting state, printed in the box.

**SOURCED — X3 Light Grey Band page:** the trigger is judged **"on an exercise"** — the full
sentence is "When you can consistently reach 40 full reps with the X3 White Band on an exercise,
the X3 Light Grey Band is your next progression." The summary bullet is even more explicit:
"move up when you can perform 40 full reps on **any** exercise."

**SOURCED — X3 White Band page** — different movements are expected to *stay* on different bands
indefinitely:

> "remains useful for experienced users on lighter movements like bicep curls and overhead press"

**SOURCED — X3 Ultra Light Band page** — per-movement band choice is endorsed explicitly:

> "for specific exercises like overhead movements where a lighter starting point makes sense"

**SOURCED — member site, Weeks 9–12 video** (already in `12-week-program.md`): "you probably
moved up a band in at least some of the exercises".

**SOURCED — the one dissenting phrasing.** The Elite Band page is the only page that gates a band
change on the whole programme rather than one lift: "across your exercises". **DERIVED:** this
reads as a purchase gate for a $100 band with over 600 lb doubled, not a change to the
progression rule. The app should treat the Elite band as a special case (see §11.6) and should
not generalise "across your exercises" to the four included bands.

**DERIVED — the app consequence.** Band state is `(movement, band, shortened?)`, not a single
user-level band. A user is routinely on three or four different bands at once. The app should
present that as normal and expected rather than as drift to be corrected — the White Band page
says so directly.

**DERIVED — the app must also carry `exercises.md`'s band ranking** so that a fresh user's
suggested starting bands are already differentiated: deadlift and bent-over row heaviest →
chest press, front squat → split squat → overhead press, bicep curl, tricep press → upright row,
pec crossover → calf raise lightest.

## 5. Q3 — Between bands: band shortening

**Important: shortening makes the band HARDER, not easier.** Getting this backwards would be the
worst error in the feature.

**SOURCED — support, "How can I shorten the X3 bands?":**

> "Many users wrap the band around the hook once or twice, which takes up some slack."

The same article describes the purpose as options "for 'shortening' the existing bands to
increase the range of motion", and offers the 34-inch Short Band Set as the productised version.

**SOURCED — member/public Chest Press page:**

> "Depending on your reach, you may want to shorten the band to extend the weak range of
> movement."

**SOURCED — X3 Short Band Set page** frames the shorter length as fit plus range:
"for users who need a shorter fit or prefer shorter bands for specific exercises".

### What this does and does not establish

- **SOURCED:** wrapping the band around the hook once or twice is an official, sanctioned
  adjustment. The mechanic is real and Jaquish Biomedical documents it.
- **SOURCED:** the stated *purpose* is **fit and range of motion** — reach, height, extending the
  weak range. It is framed as a setup adjustment.
- **UNCONFIRMED:** **no official source describes band shortening as an intermediate resistance
  step between two band colours.** Not one page says "if the next band is too heavy, shorten your
  current one instead." That framing does not exist in the official material.
- **DERIVED (high confidence, physics):** removing slack raises pre-tension and increases
  elongation at every bar position, so a shortened band is unambiguously harder than the same
  band unshortened, and the increment is smaller than a full colour change. It therefore
  *functions* as a half-step up. Consistent with `bands-and-force.md` §3: force rises
  monotonically with stretch.
- **DERIVED:** the app may offer it as **its own suggestion**, clearly labelled as the app's idea,
  citing the official mechanic. It must not say "the program recommends shortening as a half
  step."

### Going the other way — below your current band

- **SOURCED — X3 Ultra Light Band page:** it "sits below the X3 White Band in the progression".
  That is the only official sub-White option.
- **SOURCED — support, "Do you offer any lighter resistance bands?"** confirms the Ultra Light
  produces less force than White at equivalent stretch.
- **UNCONFIRMED:** whether un-doubling a band that the program prescribes doubled (chest press,
  tricep press, deadlift, bent-over row, calf raise) is an acceptable way to go lighter. No
  official source addresses it, and it would contradict the per-movement setup rule in
  `principles-and-mechanics.md` §3. **The app must not suggest it.**
- **SOURCED — stacking is discouraged for standard bands.** The X3 Performance Bands page sells
  stacking as a Performance-band-only feature: "a technique not recommended with standard X3
  bands". The band-lifespan support article adds that stacking standard bands "can cause
  premature wear that is not covered by warranty". **The app must never suggest stacking standard
  bands.**

## 6. Q4 — How firm is the downgrade rule?

**Firmer than the upgrade rule, and it is framed as a safety rule, not a programming preference.**

**SOURCED — Quick Start Guide item 6:** "Move down in resistance to a lighter band if you cannot
perform 15 slow and controlled repetitions." Stated as an unconditional instruction with no
session count, no "repeatedly", no "if this keeps happening".

**SOURCED — Dr Jaquish, pick-a-band support article:**

> "if you can't do at least 15 repetitions, you're using a weight you have no business under"

The same article gives the reason, and it is injury: he describes losing the bar, the band
snapping back into the chest on a chest press, and landing on the top of the feet on a deadlift.
He also advises against training distracted.

**SOURCED — the intro video** (already in `principles-and-mechanics.md` §4): "you're going way
too heavy and you need to drop down a band or maybe even two."

### What is and is not established

- **SOURCED:** under 15 full reps → go lighter. Unconditional, and the stated rationale is
  safety.
- **UNCONFIRMED:** whether one bad session is enough, or whether it must be a pattern. **No
  official source qualifies it either way.** Unlike the upgrade rule, no commerce page softens it
  with "consistently".
- **DERIVED:** because the source's rationale is grip failure and losing control of a loaded bar,
  the asymmetry should be respected: **the app should react on the first occurrence** rather than
  wait for a pattern. Being wrong in the "too light" direction costs one sub-optimal session;
  being wrong in the "too heavy" direction is the scenario the source describes as injurious.
- **DERIVED:** a single sub-15 session immediately after a *deliberate* band increase is the
  expected consequence of that increase, not new information. See §11.4.

## 7. Q5 — What counts toward the 40?

**Answer: full-range reps only. This is now explicitly sourced, not inferred.**

**SOURCED — support, "What bands should I use? How many reps should I do?":**

> "40 slow and controlled reps with a band, not counting partial reps"

**SOURCED — three band product pages** use the phrase **"40 full reps"** (Light Grey, Dark Grey,
Black).

**SOURCED — the member-site principle:** "40 slow, controlled **full range** reps with good form."

**SOURCED — Quick Start Guide item 2** keeps the two tiers separate in the first place: a single
set of "15–40 complete repetitions, followed by as many partial reps as possible."

### App consequence

- **DERIVED:** only reps the app classifies as **full range** (per the amplitude thresholds in
  `principles-and-mechanics.md` §1: ≥ ~0.85 of the session's established full-rep top) count
  toward 40. Mid-range and weak-range partials count toward nothing except their own celebration.
- **DERIVED:** the qualifier "slow and controlled … with good form" is also load-bearing. A rep
  under the ~4 s floor implied by the 2–3 s up / 2–3 s down tempo, or one with a detected slack
  or lockout fault, should not count toward the 40. The app already detects both. **This is the
  single biggest guard against a false "move up" recommendation:** a user who races to 40 fast
  reps has not met the stated condition.
- **DERIVED:** the bent-over row inverts the meaning of mid-range (see `exercises.md`). Its
  full-range classifier must use its own strongest position, or the app will overcount full reps
  and promote too early.

## 8. Q6 — Pacing: how long should a band last?

**UNCONFIRMED. No official source states an expected duration, rate, or number of weeks per
band.**

This was searched for specifically across the support centre, both PDFs, the band product pages,
the twelve-week phase pages already captured, and the published podcast transcripts. Jaquish
Biomedical publishes **no** statement of the form "expect to spend N weeks on a band", "you should
be moving up every N weeks", or "most users reach the Dark Grey band by week N".

Adjacent facts that exist but do **not** answer the question:

- **SOURCED — band lifespan is a durability figure, not a progression figure.** "They should last
  about 7 years before they lose enough tension to warrant replacement." This is about latex
  ageing. **The app must not repurpose it as pacing.**
- **SOURCED — the 12-week program does not schedule band changes.** None of the four phase pages
  prescribes a band for a week. The only phase-level acknowledgement is the Weeks 9–12 line, and
  it is a probabilistic observation, not a target: "you probably moved up a band in at least some
  of the exercises".
- **SOURCED — after 12 weeks**, one of the three offered paths is to repeat the program "with
  heavier bands and cleaner form". Direction, no rate.

### ANECDOTE — the one detailed public log found

A twelve-week user log (https://www.exfatloss.com/p/review-12-weeks-of-strength-training,
Dec 2023) records per-exercise band changes, starting every movement on White:

| Movement | Moved to Light Grey | Moved to Dark Grey |
|---|---|---|
| Chest press | week 2 | week 7 |
| Calf raise | week 2 | qualified week 4, actually changed week 7 |
| Tricep press | week 3 | week 9 |
| Deadlift | week 9 | — |

The same writer reports reaching no Black band on any movement in twelve weeks, and mentions an
experienced lifting friend who reached Black on some movements sooner. A second data point in the
same post: "I haven't even gotten to the 300lbs one after 3 months".

**This is one person and it is not guidance.** Its only legitimate use is calibrating how *not*
to sound: an app that implies a user is behind schedule at week 6 on White would be inventing a
standard the program does not have.

**DERIVED — what the app should say instead.** Say the honest thing: there is no schedule. The
trigger is a rep count, not a date. Movements progress at different speeds, and the source
expects that. If the app ever shows time-on-band, it should be framed as a neutral fact about the
user's own history ("you've been on White for the chest press for 5 weeks"), never compared to a
target.

## 9. Q7 — The calf raise

**SOURCED — the calf raise is officially a light-band, high-rep movement.** From the calf raise
exercise transcript (archived public mirror of the member page):

> "it's much better to do it with a lighter band and higher repetitions"

and later in the same transcript:

> "use a slightly lighter X3 band, and you will succeed with this"

The stated reasons are two: the calves respond to lower load and higher volume ("your calves are
basically contracting all day long"), and the calf raise is scheduled **after** the deadlift, so
grip is already exhausted — which is also why the bar is held against the body by the trapezius
rather than the arms.

**SOURCED — but the 40-rep rule is written to include it.** Quick Start Guide item 5 says "Start
with the lightest band **for all exercises**", and no official source carves out any exercise from
the 40-rep trigger. The Quick Start Guide's only calf-raise-specific instruction is foot position
(ball of the foot over the band channel, not midfoot).

**UNCONFIRMED: no official source says the calf raise is exempt from the 40-rep progression
rule.** There is no article, video line, or product page stating "do not move up on the calf
raise", nor any alternative rep target for it.

**DERIVED — the honest tension, and how to resolve it without inventing doctrine.** Two official
positions coexist: "lighter band, higher repetitions" for calves, and "40 reps means move up" for
all exercises. They pull in opposite directions, because the calf raise is precisely the movement
where a user will hit 40 first and most often. Following the trigger mechanically would walk the
calf raise up the band ladder until it is no longer a light-band, high-rep movement — which
defeats the stated intent of the exercise.

**DERIVED — recommendation.** Do not exempt the calf raise, because no source exempts it. Do
change the *tone*: on the calf raise, present the 40-rep event as an option accompanied by the
program's own light-band framing, offer it once, and never repeat the prompt. The app's existing
note in `exercises.md` — "its band-progression coach must not nag 'you did 40+ reps, move up' for
the calf raise" — is the right instinct and is preserved here, but it must be labelled as the
app's judgement, not the program's rule.

**Note also:** the calf raise uses a **doubled** band, so its effective load is already roughly
double the single-loop figure for its colour. A user on White doubled is not a beginner load.

## RECOMMENDED ALGORITHM

State is per `(user, movement)`. Nothing here is global.

```
BandState(movement):
  band            : one of [UltraLight, White, LightGrey, DarkGrey, Black, Elite]
  shortened       : 0 | 1 | 2      # wraps around the hook
  qualifyingCount : int            # sessions on THIS band+shortened at the 40 cap
  lastResult      : full-rep count of the most recent session
```

### 11.1 What counts as a rep (SOURCED)

A rep increments `fullReps` only if **all** hold:

1. Peak amplitude ≥ ~0.85 of the session's established full-rep top
   — *DERIVED threshold, from `principles-and-mechanics.md` §1; the requirement that reps be
   "full range" is SOURCED.*
2. Duration ≥ ~4 s (2–3 s up, 2–3 s down)
   — *SOURCED tempo; the 4 s floor is DERIVED as the low end of that stated range.*
3. No slack fault at the bottom and no lockout at the top
   — *SOURCED: "with good form", plus the constant-tension principle.*

Partials never count. **SOURCED: "not counting partial reps".**

Cap the full-rep counter at **40** and prompt the switch to partials there.
**SOURCED: "a minimum of 15 repetitions and a maximum of 40".**

### 11.2 Upgrade — two stages, because the sources give two answers

**Stage 1 — first qualifying session** (`fullReps` reaches 40 on this band):
offer the change, and attribute it. This is exactly what the printed instruction says, so the app
is on firm ground quoting it.

> **Copy:** "40 full reps. That's the number. The Quick Start Guide says: only move to the next
> heavier band when you can complete 40 slow and controlled repetitions with good form. Move up
> on the chest press next session?"
> **Buttons:** `Move up` · `Do it once more first` · `Stay here`
> **Confidence: high.** This is the program's own printed trigger.

**Stage 2 — second qualifying session** on the same band and same movement:
now recommend it rather than offer it.

> **Copy:** "Second time at 40 on the chest press. X3's own band guidance says to move up once
> you can consistently reach 40 full reps. Switching you to Light Grey."
> **Confidence: high.** Both wordings are now satisfied.

**Justification for the number two.** *DERIVED.* Two is the smallest integer that satisfies
"consistently" while never exceeding what any source requires. One is enough for the Quick Start
Guide, the support centre, the member site and Dr Jaquish himself; two is enough for the band
pages. Requiring three or more would invent a standard nobody published. The two sessions need
not be consecutive, because no source says consecutive — *UNCONFIRMED, so do not impose it.*

**Never auto-change at stage 1.** The user changes bands with their hands; the app is making a
claim, not an adjustment. An offer that the user declines should not be re-offered until the next
qualifying session.

### 11.3 Downgrade — act on the first occurrence

If `fullReps < 15` on a session, and the session was not the first session on a
just-increased band (see 11.4):

> **Copy:** "You stopped at 12 full reps. X3's rule is to move down in resistance if you can't do
> 15 slow, controlled reps — it's a safety rule as much as a training one. Drop the tricep press
> back to White?"
> **Confidence: high, and stated as safety.**

**Justification.** *SOURCED* that the rule exists and is unconditional. *DERIVED* that it should
fire on the first occurrence: the source's stated rationale is losing control of a loaded bar, and
the asymmetry of costs favours reacting early. Reset `qualifyingCount` to 0 on the new band.

Do **not** silently downgrade. Do **not** downgrade on a set the user aborted, marked as
interrupted, or logged with a fault the app flagged as equipment/setup rather than fatigue.

### 11.4 The band-change grace session (DERIVED — the app's own rule)

The first session on a newly increased band is expected to land near 15 reps. Third-party
description of the intended cycle (BarBend, *not* official): start at 15, build to 40, move up,
land back at 15.

**Rule:** suppress the downgrade prompt for the **first session** after a deliberate upgrade,
unless `fullReps < 10`. Below 10, prompt immediately with the safety framing and offer to revert.

**Justification.** *DERIVED, entirely.* Quick Start Guide items 5 and 6 collide here — item 5 sent
the user up, item 6 immediately sends them back down — and **no source arbitrates**. Without a
grace session the app would produce a visible loop, which would read as a bug and would erode
trust in every other thing the app says. The 10-rep floor is the app's own choice; label it as
such. State the reasoning to the user in one line rather than hiding it.

> **Copy:** "13 full reps on your first session with Light Grey — that's normal right after a
> band change. Give it one more session before deciding."

### 11.5 Between bands — offer shortening, honestly labelled

Offer wrapping the band around the hook when **either**:

- the user declines an upgrade twice on the same movement (the jump feels too big), **or**
- the user asks for something between the two bands.

> **Copy:** "There's a smaller step. X3 documents wrapping the band around the hook once or twice
> to take up slack — that makes your current band harder without changing colour. They describe
> it as a fit adjustment; treating it as a half-step up is this app's suggestion, not theirs."
> **Confidence: mixed, and say so.** The mechanic is SOURCED; the use as a progression step is
> the app's inference.

Track `shortened` as part of band state — a shortened White is not the same load as a plain
White, and `qualifyingCount` must reset when it changes.

**Never offer:** stacking standard bands (officially discouraged, voids wear coverage), or
un-doubling a band the program prescribes doubled (unaddressed by any source).

Below White, the only official option is the **Ultra Light Band**. Say that it is a separate
purchase.

### 11.6 The Elite band is a special case

Gate any Elite suggestion on the Black band being at 40 full reps and treat it as a purchase
decision, not a routine progression. **SOURCED:** the Elite page requires the 15–40 protocol on
Black "across your exercises", and the support article says "you should be able to do at least 40
slow and controlled reps with the included black band". Mention that it is sold separately, and
that the page itself calls it extremely challenging.

### 11.7 The calf raise

Apply the same trigger — no source exempts it — but:

- Default its suggested starting band to the **lightest** the user owns.
- On a qualifying session, use softened copy that carries the program's own framing, offer once,
  and never re-prompt for that movement on that band.

> **Copy:** "40 full reps on calf raises. You could move up — but X3's calf raise guidance is
> specifically lighter band, higher repetitions, partly because your grip is already spent from
> the deadlift. Staying on this band is a legitimate choice here."
> **Buttons:** `Move up anyway` · `Stay light` (default)
> **Confidence: deliberately lower, and visibly so.** Explain that the program's two statements
> pull different ways rather than pretending there is one answer.

### 11.8 Pacing — what the app is allowed to say

**Allowed (SOURCED or neutral fact):**

- "There's no schedule for this. The trigger is 40 full reps, not a date."
- "Different lifts move at different speeds. X3 expects that — the White band page says it stays
  useful for lighter movements like bicep curls even for experienced users."
- "You've been on Dark Grey for the deadlift since 12 March." (the user's own history, stated
  flat)

**Forbidden:**

- Any predicted or expected number of weeks per band. **UNCONFIRMED — no such figure exists.**
- Any comparison to other users, or to a norm.
- Any implication of being ahead or behind.
- Repurposing the 7-year band lifespan as a progression figure.

### 11.9 Confidence vocabulary — one register per label

| Label | Register | Example opener |
|---|---|---|
| SOURCED | Quote and attribute | "X3's Quick Start Guide says…" |
| DERIVED | Own it in the first clause | "This app's suggestion, not the program's:…" |
| UNCONFIRMED | Say so, then stop | "X3 doesn't say. Here's what's known:…" |

The single most valuable behaviour for this feature: when a user asks something the program does
not answer — *how long should this take, is once enough, should I move up on calves* — **say that
the program does not answer it.** Every one of those three questions is genuinely unanswered by
Jaquish Biomedical, and the app is more trustworthy for saying so than for filling the gap.
