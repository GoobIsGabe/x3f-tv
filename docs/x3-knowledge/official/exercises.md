## The single most important discovery: the strength curve is NOT the same shape for every movement

The app currently treats every movement as "force rises to a peak at the top of the
rep". The source says otherwise, explicitly, and it changes how a rep should be scored:

| Movement | Where the strongest range is | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Bent-Over Row | **THE MIDDLE.** "your power position is not at the top, as you would suspect. It's in the middle… the biceps and the latissimus dorsi have curves that kind of cross each other." | Peak force occurs mid-rep. A "higher = better" mapping is wrong here. Expect **10–15 partials** instead of the usual 4–5. |
| Chest Press | Near extension, short of lockout | Standard top-loaded curve |
| Tricep Press | Near extension, short of lockout ("as soon as my arm gets straight, this starts to shut off") | Standard |
| Overhead Press | Top — "head through the window", shoulders rotated back | Standard, but the **start is already at chin height under load** |
| Front Squat | Top, short of knee lockout | Standard |
| Split Squat | Top | Standard, **per leg** |
| Deadlift | Top, short of knee lockout | Standard |
| Bicep Curl | Top (bar at mid-to-lower chest, drag-curl path) | Standard |
| Calf Raise | Top (peak plantarflexion) | Standard, light band, high reps |
| Upright Row | Mid-chest — deliberately NOT to the chin | Truncated range on purpose |
| Pec Crossover | Fully crossed at the sternum | Standard |

**Partials expectation, from the source:** most movements produce "four or five"
partial reps after full-range failure. The **bent-over row produces 10–15**. The app
should not treat a big partial count on rows as an anomaly, and should not expect one
on a chest press.

---

## Universal rules that apply to every movement

- Constant tension. Never slack at the bottom, never locked out at the top.
- 2–3 seconds each direction. 15–40 full-range reps. Then diminishing-range partials
  until the bar will not move an inch.
- Thumbs wrapped, wrists neutral — **except squats**, where the trapezius holds the bar
  and the hands only steady it.
- Band centred in the **middle channel** of the ground plate — **except calf raises**,
  where the **balls of the feet** go over the band and the heels hang off the back.

# PUSH DAY

## X3 Chest Press — `chest-press`

> Multi-joint push that safely loads the stronger part of your range while protecting
> the shoulders at the bottom. Messenger-bag band setup, constant tension, finish with
> diminishing range.

**Day:** Push · **Order:** first · **Joint:** multi · **Band:** heavy

### Initial Setup
1. Hook the band onto **both** bar hooks and **double** it; if needed, wrap the band around a hook once or twice to remove slack.
2. Form a triangle with the doubled band and bar; bring the band over one shoulder like a messenger strap and rotate the bar across the body.
3. Drop the upper side of the band under the rear deltoid, thread the other arm through, and bring the bar to **chest height**.
4. Keep wrists neutral and grip the bar with a **pronated (palms-down)** hand position; wrap your thumbs around the bar.

### Execution & Mechanics
- Press forward and **slightly downward**, thinking about bringing the upper arms toward the body's midline; stop just short of lockout.
- If you feel it mostly in your triceps, keep your elbows back and slightly flared away from your body.
- Maintain constant tension — avoid letting the band go slack at the bottom.
- Slow, controlled reps (about 2–3 seconds out and 2–3 seconds back).
- As full extension becomes impossible, continue in diminishing range until the last rep is only an inch or so.

### Transcript — X3 Chest Press
The X3 chest press is done during your push workout. It makes sense to do it first, though I suppose you could do your squats first if you want to. But I like doing the chest press first because it's a multi-joint movement.

You always want to do the multi-joint movements before the single-joint movements. For example, you wouldn't want to wear out your triceps before doing the chest press. You don't want to pre-exhaust a smaller muscle which will compromise the performance of multi-joint exercises.

Now exercise order isn't the biggest thing. I tell people not to mess with the program, but you know, if you want to do your rows and bicep curls on the same day, you do your chest press and your overhead press and your tricep press, like sort of an upper-lower split versus a push-pull split, it's not the biggest problem, but on push day as we are prescribing it here, you want to start with chest press.

You're going to be working with much more force than you have previously in a gym, or you've never done regular exercise, is going to be higher weight but also a higher level of safety because you deal with a heavier weight when you're at extension, not lockout but in a more extended position because of variable resistance.

As you return, it's less weight to protect joints. Now in the diminishing range, as you shorten your reps and get back to the weaker range of motion and a more stretched muscle, you're still going to fatigue there. But you're using less weight when you go into fatigue which ensures that you're fatiguing the last little bit of muscle instead of overtaxing the joint, ultimately leading to cumulative injury.

Depending on your reach, you may want to shorten the band to extend the weak range of movement. To do so, wrap the band around the hook one or two times which takes up some slack.

We recommend choosing a band that allows you to perform anywhere between 15 to 40 repetitions at a slow pace with good form. First, hook the band on the bar and double it over. You want to start by grabbing the band, not the bar, keeping the band together, and making a triangle.

Bring the bands over your shoulder, like a messenger bag, and grab the bar with both hands and rotate until the bar is cross bodied. Drop the upper side down so the bands wrap underneath your deltoid. Drop the other arm through, and you're ready to perform the movement.

To get maximum activation of the pectorals, you need to focus on bringing your upper arm toward the midline of your body from each side. So it is that action that is using the pectorals, and when you think about it, you will build that mind-muscle connection.

You also want to push slightly downward, mimicking a decline-type press which is safer for the shoulder joint.

## X3 Tricep Press — `tricep-press`

> Maximum tricep activation while protecting the elbows with variable resistance.

**Day:** Push · **Order:** after chest press · **Joint:** single · **Band:** light–medium

### Initial Setup
1. Hook the band onto both bar hooks and double it; wrap around a hook to remove slack if needed.
2. Form a triangle; band over one shoulder like a messenger strap; rotate the bar across your body.
3. Drop the upper strand under the rear deltoid, thread the other arm through, bring the bar across the chest. **The band sits a bit higher than for the chest press.**
4. Feet about shoulder-width; **lean slightly forward (~45°)**.
5. Start with the bar at about **eyebrow / forehead level**, elbows tucked close, wrists neutral, thumbs wrapped.

### Execution & Mechanics
- Slowly straighten your arms, **hinging only at the elbows**; stop just shy of lockout.
- Keep elbows tucked — avoid flaring.
- Constant tension: no lockout, no slack.
- 15–40 slow reps, 2–3 s each direction.
- Fatigue the strong range, then diminishing-range partials through mid and weak range.

### Transcript — X3 Tricep Press
In weight lifting, the X3 tricep press movement would be called a skull crusher. I don't particularly like that name. But you start with the bar kind of forehead level, and then you push out without locking the elbow.

Notice that only this joint moves. I'm not moving this joint at all. This is frozen. I go from here to here. That's the movement for isolating the triceps. You have to keep in the pattern of exactly that movement to do this right. And that way, you're only exhausting the triceps and nothing else.

Now some people do have some issues that have a latex allergy. I have a latex allergy. So latex rubbing right here can be a little irritating for the skin. As long as I either wear a long sleeve shirt while I do the movement… I take a damp paper towel and wipe it off to get the latex powder off my skin. The allergy stops.

The setup is similar to the chest press. We end up holding the bar higher, though. Instead of chest level, you end up holding the bar kind of at eyebrow level. So a little bit higher, and then you slightly bend forward, maybe to a 45-degree angle, and begin hinging at the elbow. The only contracted muscle is the triceps, and it stays that way.

You don't move your upper arm. You hinge at the elbow and press downward. Now notice the constant tension. Slack never is put into the band. He's going to do 15 to 40 slow and controlled repetitions. Two to three seconds up. Two to three seconds down.

You will very quickly get stronger with this exercise, and you'll likely add more repetitions with every workout, if not every other one.

He's going to shorten the range to go as far as possible with every repetition. As the repetition becomes shorter, the weight becomes a little lower. He's fatiguing more and more of the muscle cells until he eventually gets to the last repetition, where he can barely move.

> **App note:** "you'll likely add more repetitions with every workout, if not every
> other one" — the tricep press is the movement where week-over-week rep progress is
> most visible. Good candidate for the progression graph the user first sees.

## X3 Overhead Press — `overhead-press`

> Targets the deltoids with constant tension and a head-through-the-window finish.

**Day:** Push · **Joint:** multi · **Band:** light–medium
**START TENSION: HIGH.** The bar starts at chin/shoulder height with the band already
stretched from the midfoot. This is the movement that broke the app's force scaling.

### Initial Setup
1. Route the **singled** band through the plate channel so it sits centered under your **midfoot**.
2. Step onto the plate, feet about shoulder-width.
3. Squat down, take a pronated grip, then **rotate your wrists 180°** (palms face down at the start, face up toward the ceiling at the top).
4. Bring the bar to shoulder height and stand tall to the start position.

### Execution & Mechanics
- Controlled cadence, about 2–3 s up and 2–3 s down.
- Press up and **slightly back** to the true top — **"head through the window"** — arms form a rectangle as shoulders rotate back and deltoids fully contract.
- **Always keep the bar in front of you. Never go behind the head** — damages the shoulder joint.
- Constant tension: don't lock out at the top, don't rest at the bottom.
- Work strong → mid → weak; diminishing-range partials to complete fatigue.
- Aim for **at least 15** slow reps. **If you can't reach 15 standing, switch to the kneeling variation** to reduce tension.

### Transcript — X3 Overhead Press
Start with the bar about chin level for the overhead press, and push it over your head. And you'll notice the model will pull their shoulders back as much as possible. I call it "head through the window." So, this is not the top. This is the top.

You want to make sure you get the strongest contraction in the deltoid. As you progress through the exercise, you want to repeat each repetition in a slow and controlled manner.

To perform the overhead press, you want the hands pronated, grabbing onto the bar. Make sure the band's running midfoot, under the ground plate. Then, you step onto the ground plate with your feet shoulder-width apart. You want to squat down to get the bar. Bring the bar up to shoulder height, and then stand up.

2 to 3 seconds up, 2 to 3 seconds down.

There's tremendous force at the top of the exercise as Kyle's pressing. But much less stress on the lower portion of the movement where the joint is compromised. Many people complain of shoulder pain… variable resistance solves that problem. But still, you want to stay with a minimum of 15 slow and controlled repetitions.

Notice how Kyle is not locking out at the top or resting at the bottom. You want to maintain constant tension so that you reach total fatigue of muscle in all ranges of motion.

Also, by keeping constant tension, you're not turning the muscle on, turning it off, and then turning it on again, which is one of the common problems with regular weight lifting. By keeping the muscles switched on through the entire movement, you're showing a true tissue deficit.

Observe the head at the top of the movement. It's almost like he's putting his head through the window. So, the arms come up, forming a rectangle. And Kyle's rotating his shoulders back to get the biggest squeeze in the deltoid possible.

Now, notice the diminishing range as Kyle begins to be unable to get to the fully contracted position. He's doing shorter and shorter repetitions until he goes to absolute fatigue.

If you're unable to perform a minimum of 15 of these repetitions, you could do the overhead press from a **kneeling position**, which puts less tension on all muscles involved.

**Sometimes individuals will start to increase the repetition speed so they can break a personal record. Don't do that.** The problem is you break form; you get sloppy. That's when you're more likely to hurt yourselves. It's better to do less repetitions but completely fatigue the muscle.

I say this all the time; **your objective is exhausting the muscle. It's not getting one more repetition, necessarily.** Yes, you should count repetitions. But you just want to fatigue the muscle as much as it can possibly be fatigued.

> **App note — this is a direct instruction to the game design.** Any mechanic that
> rewards rep *speed* is teaching the opposite of the program. Rhythm, Splash and Nova
> need auditing against this.

## X3 Front Squat — `front-squat`

> Loads the body where you're strongest while keeping joints safe in the weaker range.

**Day:** Push · **Joint:** multi · **Band:** heavy

### Initial Setup
1. Center the **singled** band in the plate's channel and loop **both ends over the bar hooks**.
2. Rest the bar **across the top of the shoulders**; elbows slightly forward and up; stand tall to set it.
3. Stand hip- to shoulder-width; toes slightly out so knees and toes track together.

### Execution & Mechanics
- **Light fingertip/thumb contact is enough — your trapezius holds the bar, not your hands.**
- Torso upright, chest up; bar balanced across the shoulders.
- **Sit straight down and straight up** — "sit into a chair" keeps the path vertical.
- Track knees with toes; don't let knees cave inward.
- No lockout at the top; smooth constant tension.
- After 15–40 full reps, shorten the range to complete fatigue.

### Transcript — X3 Front Squat
The front squat is biomechanically superior to the back squat. When you have something heavy to carry, you don't normally say, let me put it at the top of my spine and balance it behind my head so I can walk around with this heavy object. Nobody would ever say that. Yet that is the norm for squatting in a normal fitness environment.

You're going to start with the front squat, but then you will graduate to the split squat, which is superior.

For the X3 front squat, you place the ground plate over the top of the band, making sure the band is in the middle of the channel. Next, loop the bands around the hook on each side of the bar. You pull the bar under the top of your shoulders and stand up slowly. The elbow should be pointed forward and slightly up. This keeps the bar balanced across the shoulders and the clavicle. You can retain the bar with your thumbs or just your fingertips.

The feet should be about hip to shoulder width apart and turned slightly outward. If you feel like you need to have your hands on the bar, it probably means you're leaning too far forward or back, and the bar could slip.

Notice how Sarah is dropping her hips straight down like she is going to be sitting in an imaginary chair at the bottom. Practicing with a chair right behind you is not the worst thing in the world.

Another good cue is to look down at your kneecaps and try and see them in line from your eyes to your big toe. So that knee and the toe should always be in line as you move up and down. Sarah avoids locking her knees at the top of the movement. **This keeps constant tension because when you lock your knees, you're resting and only loading the bone. Very little muscle activity happens.**

## X3 Pec Crossover — `pec-crossover` *(optional, weeks 5+)*

> Optional chest-isolation finisher **after** chest press.

**Day:** Push · **Joint:** single · **Band:** light · **Unlocks:** week 5

### Initial Setup
1. Wrap a **looped band behind your upper back at mid-scapula level**, strands crossing over the rear deltoids; hold one side of the loop in each hand.
2. Staggered stance, lean slightly forward, brace the core, neutral spine.
3. Arms out to the sides at chest height, soft elbow bend; wrists neutral, palms slightly inward.
4. **Pre-tension the band (no slack)** by stepping forward or adjusting hand spacing before the first rep.

### Execution & Mechanics
- Sweep the arms in a smooth arc across the body, meeting at the sternum/midline; **alternate which arm crosses on top each rep**.
- Constant tension — no rest at start or finish.
- Shoulders down and back, no shrug; soft elbow — it's a fly, not a press.
- 2–3 s each way, no momentum.
- Don't lock out at the finish.
- Diminishing-range partials until the band no longer moves forward.

### Transcript — X3 Pec Crossover
The pectoral crossover is a bit of a supplementary movement. It is not required. And it's really for somebody who wants some serious thickness in their pectorals.

You want to perform the movement **immediately after doing the chest press**. You've pre-exhausted the chest, but you're also using the deltoids and the triceps in the chest press. With the pec crossover, you're focusing on just the absolute shutdown of the pectorals in isolation.

You want to ensure that you're not just sticking your arms out and stretching the band. You're truly crossing the body. I like pointing out that you should probably align one elbow joint over the top of the other elbow joint. And then you alternate with the next repetition.

You want to get the biggest squeeze you can in the pectorals, in their shortest position to make sure you're evacuating all of the ATP, glycogen and creatine phosphate from the muscles.

The setup: bring the band over your head and behind your back. The band should cross at the center of your deltoid muscles. The action is pushing across the body arm over arm alternating one repetition to the next.

For example, in a bench press-type movement, you really don't get the opportunity to take them to the completely contracted position, which is why we have this as an addition after the chest press.

## X3 Split Squat — `split-squat` *(optional, weeks 5+)*

> Unilateral lower-body movement loading the front leg through constant tension.

**Day:** Push · **Joint:** multi · **Band:** medium · **Unlocks:** week 5 · **PER LEG**

### Initial Setup
1. Hook the band onto both sides of the bar.
2. Step your **front foot onto the band so it runs under the front foot near the heel**; step the rear foot back into a stable split stance (rear heel high).
3. Because the band runs under only one foot, there is no concern it will twist the ankle inward.
4. Rest the bar across your shoulders as in the front squat — fingertips only, trapezius holds it.
5. Square the hips to the front and brace the core. Torso upright, chest up.

### Execution & Mechanics
- Drive upward with body weight **over the front leg**; the rear leg is balance only.
- Descend under control until the **back knee nearly touches the floor** — straight down, not forward.
- About two seconds up, two seconds down.
- Expect strong core stabilisation; torso upright, hips level.
- **15–40 full reps per leg**; then shorten the range.
- Finish with very short reps near the bottom.

### Transcript — X3 Split Squat
The split squat is superior to the front squat, which is far superior to a back squat. Our own biomechanics are better when we use one leg at a time, probably because we're humans and we walk on one leg at a time, we run on one leg at a time. So focusing all the body's resources on one quadricep and one glute, as opposed to both — everything from your heart, your lungs, all your resources going into just one side of your body, taking that to complete exhaustion, and then the other side.

Begin with a band hooked on each side of the bar. The band should run underneath your foot and close to the heel. Then bring the bar up to your shoulders, just like the regular squat. You want to drive upwards, putting your body weight over the front leg. The rear leg is only there for balance purposes.

As Alex descends, notice her knee almost touches the ground. Same cadence, two seconds up, two seconds down. There's a lot of stabilization activity through the core. This stabilization activity builds a higher level of circulating growth hormone during the action and afterward.

As she starts to go to fatigue, with a complete range of motion, 15 to 40 repetitions with each leg, she starts shortening the repetitions. She's going to go until the repetitions are so short she's almost just kneeling on the ground with complete exhaustion.

You'll be done with the split squat, and you'll probably feel it more or less the most in the core… because of the stabilization required.

> **App note:** the split squat is the only movement that is **per side**. The set model,
> the timer, the rest logic and the log entry all need a `perSide: true` flag — two
> sets, one per leg, counted as one movement.

## X3 Upright Row — `upright-row` *(optional — substitute for overhead press)*

> A vertical pull alternative **for those who cannot perform the overhead press**.

**Day:** Push · **Joint:** multi · **Band:** light · **Role: SUBSTITUTE, not an addition**

### Initial Setup
1. Loop the **singled** band under the center of the ground plate and hook both ends onto the bar.
2. Stand centered on the plate, feet hip- to shoulder-width; soften the knees, brace the core.
3. Grip evenly just inside shoulder width, wrists neutral, thumbs around; stand tall.

### Execution & Mechanics
- Initiate by driving the **elbows up and slightly out** — wrists below elbows, bar close; shoulders stay down and back, don't shrug early.
- Pull vertically until the bar reaches about **mid-chest. No higher.**
- Upright torso; no leaning, no momentum.
- 2–3 s each direction, constant tension.
- Diminishing-range partials as full range breaks down.

### Transcript — X3 Upright Row
Now, the upright row is **only, I repeat, only** for people who lack shoulder mobility that cannot do the overhead press. So there are some people who have damaged their shoulder joints… And if people can't do this, what choice do you have? So the upright row is a great alternative.

Now, biomechanically the upright row is not the best exercise for a whole host of reasons. But if that's the only mobility you have, that's what you're left with.

You end up grabbing the bar and then pulling up, and you want to get to about mid-chest level at the top. You'll see people doing upright rows where they get the bar right up at their chin, **don't do that**. Because remember, you're doing this because you're compromised.

The setup is the same as the overhead press. Standing on the ground plate, band running midfoot, hands on the bar with a narrow-width grip.

Anyone who has chosen to do this movement, chances are you have shoulder issues. With a limited range of motion the shoulder is compromised, and you want to start with a very light amount of resistance. So the lighter bands are preferred.

As you cannot get to the mid-chest anymore, you want the mid-range repetitions, they may end in your abdominal area until you can no longer move.

> **App note:** the library must present the upright row as a **substitution for the
> overhead press**, gated behind "I can't do the overhead press", not as a free extra
> push movement. Presenting it as an equal option is a form-safety miss.

## X3 Deadlift — `deadlift`

> Posterior chain movement combining traditional and Romanian deadlift elements.

**Day:** Pull · **Order:** first · **Joint:** multi · **Band:** heaviest

### Initial Setup
1. **Double** the band under the ground plate, centered in the middle channel.
2. Grip the bar with an **overhand (double pronated)** grip aligned with your mid-foot and pull the slack out of the band.
3. Feet hip-width apart, **toes at the front edge of the plate** so mid-foot aligns with the band, knees slightly bent.
4. Back straight from neck to pelvis before you begin.

### Execution & Mechanics
- Constant tension: **reverse just before the band would go slack** at the bottom; no bouncing, no momentum.
- Head and eyes forward throughout; don't turn your head.
- **Hinge at the hips** and drive the bar up **using your glutes**; bar tracks close to the legs the whole time.
- Keep traps engaged so shoulders don't slump forward; avoid rounding the back.
- **Do not lock out the knees** at the top.
- 2–3 seconds each way to stay in control through the weaker range.
- When full-range reps fail, finish with lengthened partials.

### Transcript — X3 Deadlift
So the deadlift, from a full-body functional standpoint, is probably the most important exercise humans can do. It involves some of the most difficult muscles to engage with weights. A lot of people don't do the deadlift because there are a lot of injuries associated with it. But with X3, because of variable resistance, the chances of injuries are greatly diminished.

I have two hemorrhaged discs from rugby when I was in university. So, I don't feel any pain in my back whatsoever… even with those chronic injuries, I can't feel them because I've built so much muscle in my back to support my spine.

Now, a couple of keys to this. **First, don't use straps.** Your grip strength is a limit on what you can do in your deadlift. Leave it that way. When you use straps, you get sort of a supernatural loading on your back that you couldn't get with just your grip. Your grip is a limit to what you can place on your back. And it's a limit for a reason. If you want to be able to grip it better, do the movement without straps, and you'll build your grip strength.

Now, we **don't recommend the switch grip** or the Olympic grip. We do a **double overhand** because when it gets too heavy, you can just put the bar down. The scapula are in two different positions when you have an Olympic grip, and that ultimately gives you uneven biomechanics. We'd rather you have both hands pronated.

To set up, put the doubled band on the ground and the ground plate on top of it, aligning the middle of the ground plate with the middle of the band. Grab the bar with an overhand grip, with the band aligned with the middle of the foot. Once you step on the platform, make sure your toes are at the edge or the front of the plate so that your center of gravity becomes the middle of the plate. As you begin to move, make sure your back is straight, not curved in either direction, but straight from the base of the neck to the pelvis.

At no point in this exercise should there be any slack in the band. **Keep in mind you're not pulling the bar from the ground but from just below the knee.** So at the bottom position, you want to keep a little bit of tension, and then before you lose tension, you reverse direction and start the next repetition.

Many who are doing a standard weightlifting deadlift try and get by the weaker range of motion very quickly… they'll bounce the weight off the ground. **When you're using variable resistance, there's no momentum. Zero.** The abrupt movements add to the risk. So it may be better to pick a lighter band and go slowly through that weaker range of motion.

She isn't pulling with her arms or really her lower back, she's hinging at the hips and driving the bar up with her glutes. Stabilizing muscles are firing all over the body. The hamstrings, the quadratus muscles, even muscles in your neck.

You also want to make sure that the trapezius muscles are always engaged in controlling the shoulders so they don't slump forward, because once they do, the back will start rounding. That is poor form, and that could cause an injury. You also do not lock out the knees at the top.

**Don't ignore the diminishing range with this.** Sometimes when people go to fatigue with full repetitions, it's easy to just put the bar down at the end of that movement. And this is true of all movements, but it's harder with the deadlift because more musculature throughout your entire body is being used. You've got to stay motivated. You've got to truly go to fatigue in all ranges of motion. It's very hard to do. I understand why people quit early, but I'm telling you, **see it through to the end because that's the part that counts the most.**

> **App note:** "that's the part that counts the most" is the single best justification
> for the app's headline metric being **partials past failure**, not total reps. The
> deadlift is also the movement most likely to be quit early — a good place for the
> app's strongest "don't stop" moment.

## X3 Bent-Over Row — `bent-over-row`

> Targets the lats and posterior deltoids while minimizing arm involvement.

**Day:** Pull · **Joint:** multi · **Band:** heavy
**STRONGEST RANGE: THE MIDDLE.** Expect 10–15 partials, not 4–5.

### Initial Setup
1. **Double** the band under the ground plate, centered through the channel.
2. Grip the bar with a **supinated (underhand)** grip; pronated optional based on comfort.
3. Stand shoulder-width on the plate so mid-foot aligns with the band, knees slightly bent.
4. **Hinge at the hips to ~45°** with a flat, neutral spine.
5. Let the bar hang below the knees with arms long, elbows pointed back.

### Execution & Mechanics
- Drive the elbows back to pull the bar **toward the beltline**, keeping it close to the body.
- **Don't pull into your chest** or a back injury can occur. The closer to the hip, the less stress on the back.
- Initiate with the lats and upper back, not the arms.
- Constant tension; avoid straightening the arms at the bottom.
- 2–3 seconds up and down.
- **Strongest range is mid-rep — expect many mid-range partials**; then shorter reps into the weak range.

### Transcript — X3 Bent-Over Row
So the bent row is primarily for the latissimus dorsi… It runs the course or sort of side of your back. Covers all the way into the ribcage and the posterior deltoid. There is some bicep work, but not nearly the bicep work that is involved with the actual X3 bicep curl. This is a multi-joint movement.

You want to focus on feeling it in your back, but you also want to keep a neutral spine. You don't want to be hunched over. Not overly arched, not hunched, but flat back. So if somebody looks at you from the side, your back looks like a straight line at an angle.

One thing that is very unique about this… is that **you will go to complete fatigue with many more partial repetitions over all other movements in the X3 program. And the reason for this is the strength curve. With a rowing-type movement, your power position is not at the top, as you would suspect. It's in the middle. And it's because the biceps and the latissimus dorsi have curves that kind of cross each other.** It just happens that the power position is in the middle of the movement, as opposed to the top.

So what you do is you might do 15 movements that get you all the way to the top, and then the rest of the repetitions will be partials, but there will be a lot of them. So instead of two or three, as there may be with other movements, you might have six or seven, by comparison.

For the bent row, make sure the band is doubled underneath the ground plate, in line with the midfoot. You want to grab the bar with the hands supinated.

As always, slow and controlled repetitions, two seconds up and two seconds down. The strongest range of motion in this movement is unlike the other movements. **It is the middle that is the most powerful part of this movement.** So you want to do 15 to 40 full-range repetitions, but instead of having four or five partial repetitions, as with most other exercises, **you may have another 10 to 15 partial repetitions in this mid-range** of motion, because you still have so much power left in that position, as it is actually the strongest.

## X3 Bicep Curl — `bicep-curl`

> Drag-curl motion to isolate the biceps and keep tension high through the strongest range.

**Day:** Pull · **Joint:** technically multi (traps/rear delt involved) · **Band:** light–medium

### Initial Setup
1. Loop the band over both bar hooks and **single** it over the ground plate's center channel.
2. **Supinated (palms-up)** grip; elbows **slightly flexed**, wrists neutral to maintain constant tension.
3. Stand on the plate centered over the band, feet shoulder-width.

### Execution & Mechanics
- Curl by **flexing your elbows only**; keep the bar close to the body; **elbows move backward as the bar comes up**, never forward.
- **Drag-curl path:** slide the bar up along the torso; bar travels straight up and down.
- Lift until the bar reaches **mid-to-lower chest** — full contraction. Not higher.
- 2–3 s each direction.
- Constant tension; don't lock out at the bottom.
- Diminishing range to complete fatigue.

### Transcript — X3 Bicep Curl
The bicep curl that we recommend, and really this is the only way to do any bicep curl, is called the drag curl. So you're keeping the bar close to your body.

The reason is when you do a standard curl, at this point the center of gravity is close to the elbow joint, and you're actually not loading the bicep very much at all right here.

Instead, you keep the X3 Bar closer to your body, which is how you would normally pick up anything heavy. Then pull it up. So that way, at the top position, you have a very hard contraction and a lot of resistance into the bicep at the top.

The bottom is not with your arms perfectly straight, but there's a slight bend to them, so you keep a partial contraction for constant tension purposes. Place the ground plate on the ground and **single** the band through the bottom. So not doubled, singled.

As you pull the bar upwards, don't do this like a traditional curl. This is a drag curl. Your elbows go back. Your hands don't go out in front of you.

When you move the bar up, notice where he stops at the top. Just sort of mid to lower chest, and that's where the biceps are fully contracted. **You don't want to go any higher than this because if you do, you break the isolation of the biceps.**

If you watch closely, the bar follows a straight up-and-down path. Very slow and controlled. Two to three seconds up, two or three seconds down. Squeezing the biceps hard when getting to the top establishes a great mind-muscle connection.

There is constant tension, so no resting at the bottom. No locking out at the top; there's also no way to do that with the biceps anyway.

### Transcript — "Why is the X3 bicep curl different than a typical bicep curl?"
A normal curl position finishes by bringing the bar almost up to the bridge of your nose. The problem with bringing the bar right here is if you look at the radius and ulna, the lower arm is perpendicular to the ground. **When this happens, the bicep is shut off.** So you have a situation where you're turning the muscle on, turning it off, turning it on, turning it off — it's a terrible stimulus. You're not keeping constant tension.

So we get the highest tension and the hardest contraction right here [mid-chest].

The reason they call it drag curl is 'cause you're hovering the bar, kind of dragging it up the front of your body. As you do this, you're engaging the posterior deltoid, but also your traps — the trapezius kind of pushes your head a little forward, and that's normal.

Now, it also turns the movement into a **multi-joint** movement, and what we know from research is that multi-joint movements stimulate more growth than single-joint movements. By getting another joint involved, we're enabling the body to see two joints moving, maximizing the musculature that could be involved.

## X3 Calf Raise — `calf-raise`

> Gastrocnemius and soleus with continuous tension and high-rep fatigue.

**Day:** Pull · **Order:** last · **Joint:** single · **Band:** LIGHTEST · **Reps:** highest

### Initial Setup
1. **Double** the band under the ground plate, centered in the channel, and loop onto both bar hooks.
2. **Place the ball of each foot directly over the band in the channel, heels hanging off the back of the plate**; stand hip-width for balance. *(This is the one exercise that is NOT midfoot.)*
3. Start in a deadlift stance, pull up on the bar, stand tall with control.
4. **Hold the bar firmly against your body using the trapezius, not your arms.**
5. Ensure balance before starting — this exercise challenges stability.

### Execution & Mechanics
- Keep the bar against the body; contract the trapezius to stabilise the shoulder joint.
- **Higher reps with a lighter band** — focus on smooth range and control.
- **Hover the heels off the back of the plate at the bottom** to maintain constant tension; the heels should never touch the plate.
- As soon as you can no longer reach peak contraction, finish with lengthened partials.

### Transcript — X3 Calf Raise
Contrary to popular belief, you can grow your calves. Some people have said in the past — a common weightlifter/bodybuilder myth — that if you want great calves, you should be born with great calves. This is not true. I never really had calves. And now it looks like somebody glued a ribeye on the back of my lower legs.

Higher repetitions with the calves — your calves are basically contracting all day long, so that kind of makes sense. The body responds to lower weight and higher repetitions. Also, you've probably done the deadlift first or another exercise that would exhaust your grip. So it's much better to do it with a lighter band and higher repetitions.

**You do not want the band to be midfoot, like some of the other movements. You want the balls of your feet over the band channel in the plate.** That way, the center of your body is really focused on the balls of your feet. You'll notice that you're almost pitched slightly forward to compensate.

She doubles the band on the ground and then places the ground plate over the top, then hooks the bar to the band. The ball of the foot should be centered directly over the band channel. You want the heels hanging off the back of the ground plate. The heels should not touch the ground plate during the movement.

You're gonna be moving up and down, but you don't wanna relax at the bottom. As soon as she can no longer reach the peak point of contraction, she starts limiting her range of motion and goes through the diminishing range process until complete fatigue.

> **High rep counts are the point on this movement, not a signal to add load.**
